What it does

A verifier posts an ask — show me you cleared £250. The holder answers with one sentence backed by their own receipts. The chain holds a verdict and a nullifier. It does not hold the money.

“✓ Met £250.00. Checked against the anchored record.”

Receipts become a hash-chained run of checkpoints, anchored in a Merkle tree. A statement is a zero-knowledge proof that the run leads to the anchored root and clears the threshold. It answers exactly once.

The privacy claim, measured

Read the ledger directly, with its own client, not through the app. These are the only fields a verdict carries.

On chain: met, threshold, concentrationOk, batches, receiptCount, verifierPk

Never on chain: amounts, counterparties, totals, the holder’s key

The threshold is on the verdict on purpose. Without it a statement never says which amount it answers, and an answer to a penny reads identically to an answer to a thousand pounds. It is the verifier’s own figure and was already public.

What a verifier does learn: the number they asked for, and whether it was met. Repeated asks at different thresholds narrow a total by bisection — inherent to answering at all, and stated rather than hidden.

What cannot be faked

Every assert in the circuit was forced to fire. None can be reached through the interface, because the interface builds honest witnesses — so the tests lie to the contract directly.

Each refusal has a sentence the app can show a person. Before this work, not one had ever been produced — a message nobody has seen fire is a guess.
